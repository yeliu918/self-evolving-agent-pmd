Unpacking the Compute Efficiency of
Strong-to-Weak OPD against GRPO

On-policy distillation trains a student on its own rollouts under a frozen teacher's per-token supervision. Does that dense signal actually reach a target accuracy in fewer training FLOPs than reinforcement learning? We answer with the first systematic, matched-FLOPs comparison against GRPO.

Zeyu Leo Liu1, Yang Li1, Bo Pang1, Emre Can Acikgoz1,2, Xuan-Phi Nguyen1, Anurag Koul1, Srijan Bansal1, Erik Nijkamp1, Shafiq Joty1, Semih Yavuz1

TL;DR

With an off-the-shelf teacher, vanilla reverse-KL OPD is not more compute-efficient than GRPO — it usually loses, and inflates response length. Among the teachers we tried, OPD becomes dramatically efficient in one case: when the teacher is the student's own GRPO checkpoint (a self-GRPO teacher). That reframes OPD's real value — not as a replacement for GRPO, but as a cheap consolidation step that folds several per-domain GRPO experts into one model, beating joint GRPO at lower total compute.

Three findings

Where OPD falls short of GRPO — and where it wins decisively

We measure held-out accuracy (mean@16) against cumulative training FLOPs, an implementation-invariant compute measure, while varying data difficulty, student and teacher size and initialization, domain, thinking mode, the distillation objective, and the RL framework.

FINDING 01

External teachers don't pay off

Across a nine-axis sweep, vanilla reverse-KL OPD with an off-the-shelf post-trained teacher is broadly less compute-efficient than running GRPO directly — even when the teacher is itself GRPO-trained, and even in the regime that should favor OPD most. It also pathologically inflates response length.

FINDING 02

The self-GRPO teacher makes OPD compute-efficient

Of the teacher types we tested, OPD reliably recovers the teacher's accuracy only when the teacher is a GRPO checkpoint of the student itself. In the math setting it matches GRPO at 50× fewer distillation-stage FLOPs; the pattern of full recovery (not the exact ratio) also holds across domains, student sizes, and model families.

FINDING 03

Specialize with GRPO, then consolidate with OPD

On a single domain, distilling a checkpoint you already have is a paradox. Across domains it's an opportunity: multi-teacher OPD merges per-domain GRPO experts into one student, adding only 5–8% to specialist FLOPs and beating joint GRPO.

Setup & compute accounting

Same axis for both methods: accuracy per training FLOP

Past efficiency claims rely on GPU-hours or step counts, which depend on the implementation, and never compare OPD and GRPO at matched FLOPs. We do: every run is priced in FLOPs per generated token, which is implementation-invariant. Here, Ns is the number of parameters in the student model, and Nt is the number of parameters in the teacher model.

GRPO10·Ns per generated token  =  2 (generate) + 2 (log-prob recompute) + 6 (update)
OPD adds+ 2·Nt — one frozen-teacher forward pass over every training token

So fewer rollouts or steps don't guarantee fewer FLOPs — OPD also pays for the teacher pass and tends to generate longer responses. Its loss is a closed-form reverse KL over the student's top-k tokens (k=16) on its own rollouts; the outcome reward never enters it.

  • Students. Qwen3 1.7B / 4B / 8B — mostly base checkpoints, plus selected SFT cold starts.
  • Teachers. External thinking models (Qwen3 4B–32B), GRPO-trained base models, further-GRPO-trained thinking models, and self-GRPO checkpoints of the student.
  • Math. Train on DAPO-Math-17K & DeepMath-103k; evaluate OOD on AIME24 + AIME25 + AMC23.
  • Code. Train on LiveCodeBench v1–v5 + CodeForces; evaluate on LiveCodeBench v6.
  • Multi-domain. Adds MMLU-Pro (knowledge) and IFBench (instruction following).
  • Metric. mean@16 — mean accuracy over 16 samples per prompt.
Finding 01 · in depth

External teachers are compute-inefficient for OPD

Under the vanilla reverse-KL objective, GRPO sits above every external-teacher OPD run at every evaluated FLOP count. Larger teachers only push OPD's curve rightward (more FLOPs) without lifting the student's accuracy.

Qwen3-1.7B-Base, 3 runs each: GRPO leads at every FLOP count; OPD then collapses

Qwen3-1.7B-Base student (SFT-initialized); OPD distills from an external Qwen3-8B teacher. Macro mean@16 over AIME24 / AIME25 / AMC23 vs. cumulative training FLOPs (1 EFLOP = 1018); higher is better. Line = mean of 3 runs, band = ±1 std, faint lines = individual runs.

FLOPs count the tokens each run actually processed: 10·Nstudent per token, plus 2·Nteacher for OPD's teacher forward pass. With the teacher pass and longer rollouts, a full OPD run costs 2.9× a full GRPO run (23.7 vs. 8.3 EFLOP).

Code to reproduce this plot: github.com/leo-liuzy/unpack_opd_vs_grpo.

  • GRPO leads at every FLOP count. On Qwen3-1.7B-Base (3 runs each), GRPO stays above OPD with an 8B teacher across the whole compute range: at ~2 EFLOP, GRPO is at 12.8% vs. OPD's 11.5%. GRPO reaches 18.7% using 8.3 EFLOP; OPD peaks at 14.5% after 14.0 EFLOP (1.7× more), then collapses in all 3 runs to ~0.5%.
  • Picking the "right" training prompts doesn't help. We binned prompts by how often the student and the teacher solve them (an 8-cell difficulty grid) and trained on each cell separately: GRPO wins in-domain in every cell — even where the student barely solves anything and the teacher solves most.
  • Nine axes, same verdict. Student init, domain, dataset difficulty, learning rate, batch size, reverse-KL estimator, thinking mode, student and teacher size, and RL framework — none flips the ordering.
  • One fragile exception -- rollout group size G=1. On math alone, OPD from Qwen3-8B then beats GRPO at matched FLOPs, though it needs 8× as many optimizer steps. On code it still loses, and on the 4-domain mix it collapses.

The loop probe is conservative: it flags a rollout when the 100-char window response[-200:-100] recurs ≥ 3 times, so it catches short token-level loops but misses longer or paraphrased repetition. The de-looped lengths (cut at the first repeat) are therefore an upper bound on unique content — and even so, the external-teacher median is ~4.6× GRPO's.

Finding 02 · in depth

Efficient only when the teacher is the student's own RL checkpoint

We sweep the teacher along three axes — base vs. thinking init, used as-is vs. further GRPO-trained, and same-size vs. larger. Among these, one setup stands out: the self-GRPO teacher, the student's own GRPO checkpoint.

The teacher design space: three axes, one efficient teacher

Student fixed: Qwen3-4B-Base · DAPO-Math-17K, thinking mode. We vary only the teacher, on three axes — init (rows), training (columns), and size (the 4B / 8B / 14B chips inside each family). In each chip the large number is the OPD student’s held-out OOD-math accuracy (macro mean@16) using that teacher; the small line below is the training compute to get there (EFLOP) or the post-peak trend. Plain GRPO on this student reaches 33.9% — the baseline an OPD student must outperform for its teacher to be worth using. A raw base used as-is (top-left) is too weak to be an appealing teacher — its own accuracy falls short of that mark — so we run OPD only from the three tested families.

  • One efficient, stable teacher. Base-initialized, GRPO-trained, and the same size as the student — i.e. the student’s own GRPO checkpoint (“self-GRPO”): it recovers GRPO’s 33.9% at 1.62 EFLOP (~6× cheaper; ~50× at group size G=1) and even peaks at 35.8%.
  • 135.6× cheaper than an external teacher. Same student, same OPD loss — only the teacher changes. To reach GRPO’s ~34%, an off-the-shelf 8B thinking teacher needs 27.2 EFLOP; the self-GRPO teacher gets there from ~0.2 EFLOP (G=1). The efficiency comes from the teacher being the student’s own RL checkpoint, not from dense supervision per se.
  • Larger teachers don’t transfer. Scaling the teacher to 8B or 14B does not lift the OPD student above the same-size 4B teacher.
  • Off-the-shelf thinking teachers are slow. Used as-is, a thinking teacher pays a heavy per-step compute cost.
  • Further-GRPO thinking teachers collapse. Continuing to GRPO-train a thinking teacher degrades the OPD student.
High compute-efficiency with self-GRPO generalizes

A self-GRPO teacher fully recovers GRPO accuracy at a fraction of the compute well beyond the 4B math run — for an 8B-Base student, for code, and for Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT.

The most striking case is Olmo-3-7B-Instruct-SFT: distilling the student’s own GRPO checkpoint, the self-GRPO student doesn’t just match its teacher — it clearly surpasses it, and does so on a sliver of the compute.

Finding 03 · in depth

Multi-teacher OPD: the natural use case

On one domain, distilling a checkpoint you already trained is a paradox. Across domains it becomes an opportunity: separate GRPO experts each hold different capabilities, and no single checkpoint serves them all. Multi-teacher OPD routes each instance to its domain's self-GRPO teacher and merges them into one student.

  • Beats joint GRPO. Self-GRPO OPD (multi-teacher) matches joint GRPO with 42.0% fewer FLOPs on 2 domains and 31.6% fewer on 4 — joint GRPO underperforms within budget.
  • Cheap consolidation. Specialists cost 10.7 EFLOP (2 domains) / 14.4 EFLOP (4 domains); the merge adds only 0.55 / 1.15 EFLOP (about 5.2% / 8.0%).
  • Three practical wins. Better overall accuracy, modular development (each domain keeps its own stack, rewards, and hyper-parameters), and parallelizable specialist training.
  • A rationale for frontier practice. Nemotron-Cascade 2, DeepSeek-V4, and Kimi K3 already merge domain experts this way — we supply the matched-compute justification they omit.
One external 8B teacher for every domain: the student collapses

OPD with one strong external 8B-thinking teacher is the worst option: it reaches only 26.7% on 2 domains, and on 4 domains the student collapses on most domains — with severe response-length inflation and degenerate output.

The takeaway

OPD isn't a replacement for GRPO — it's the merge step

Dense per-token supervision alone does not make on-policy distillation cheaper than GRPO. Its real, reproducible compute advantage appears when the teacher is a GRPO checkpoint of the student, and it pays off at the pipeline level: train per-domain GRPO specialists in parallel, then consolidate them into a single generalist with multi-teacher OPD — a model no individual specialist provides, at lower total compute than training everything jointly.

Scope & limitations

Results concern the vanilla reverse-KL OPD objective; variants we don't sweep at matched FLOPs (hybrid outcome-reward objectives, alternative divergences, reshaped rollouts) may narrow the gap. The negative result is about this objective, not OPD in general.
